Franchise Lead Follow-Up With AI: The Complete Guide

By Zehaan Khan · Updated 2026-11-08 · 14 min read

The short version

  • Call first. Calls get picked up more and qualify people faster than text or email.
  • Call at least three times. 30% of the people we reached only answered on a later attempt.
  • Only 37% of calls reach a live person, so check what any "connect rate" counts.
  • Put your brand name on caller ID, keep one question per turn, and have a person ready to step in.

This guide covers everything between a franchise inquiry and a booked intro call. It comes from what we built and ran for a franchise consulting firm: 5,379 AI calls from January 2025 to September 2026, including what broke.

We've been building AI automation since 2023 and franchise lead systems since 2024. We're also a certified Retell AI partner, so factor that in when we talk about Retell.

What should happen in the first hour?

A new lead should get a real attempt to reach them within minutes. Most brands don't manage it.

Those leads cost an average of $351 each. A slow first touch wastes money you've already spent.

For our firm, the moment a form is filled in, the AI calls. It confirms who it's talking to, checks it's a good time, qualifies them and sets up the next step.

How fast do you need to be?

As fast as you legally can, but speed has limits. Being quick gets you the conversation. It doesn't sign the deal, and a franchise sale still takes about 24 weeks.

The numbers people quote are often wrong too. We break those down in what the speed to lead data actually says.

We haven't published our own average time to first dial, because we haven't finished matching every call to the moment each lead arrived.

Should you call, text or email first?

We call first. That goes against most franchise advice. FranFunnel, for example, recommends text first, then call, then email.

Here's our reasoning. Calls get picked up more, and a conversation is the fastest way to qualify someone. In our experience, texts and emails mostly get ignored, so they come last.

To be fair, we never ran a controlled test of call first against text first. This is what we chose and what we saw.

How many times should you call?

At least three times. Pick-up falls with each attempt, but later calls still reach people the first one missed.

Stop after one call and you lose nearly a third of the people you would have reached. Across every attempt, the AI eventually reached 70% of leads (1,214 of 1,733) on a live call.

Timing seemed to matter a little. Roughly 35% of morning calls reached someone, against about 44% from mid-afternoon to evening. Mondays did better than Fridays, at about 44% and 34%. Treat those as rough, because leads were in different time zones and calls went out when leads arrived.

What really happens when an AI calls?

Fewer than 4 in 10 calls reach a live person.

Our first report said 77% of calls connected, and we believed it. That number counted voicemails. The honest figure is 37%. If you're buying or building this, ask how "connected" is defined.

The same goes for call length. The average across every call was 41 seconds, because it includes voicemails. Live calls of a minute or more typically ran about 2.4 minutes.

Does caller ID matter?

Yes, and almost nobody talks about it. Our first calls showed a generic phone number. When we switched to showing the brand name, pick-ups improved.

We only ran unbranded for about two weeks, so we can't give you a reliable number. Set it up from day one.

How do people react to an AI caller?

Better than you'd expect. One lead answered with:

"Oh my god. The robocall."A real lead, on answering the phone. He then stayed on and finished the call.

The agent told him it was an AI, and he stayed on and answered every question. On other calls, people couldn't believe it was AI and seemed to enjoy it.

Those sentiment scores come from the AI's own analysis, so they're a guide and not a survey.

Some people will want a human, and you need a way to give them one. We positioned the AI as a first touch with a person ready to step in. That's also why the team didn't push back: it wasn't replacing anyone.

What should the AI ask?

Only what you need to decide what happens next. Our agent covers nine things, one per turn.

Below your minimum credit score or investment, the AI politely says it isn't a fit. Decide those minimums before you write the prompt.

Structure beats clever wording

Our early prompts were one long block. The agent would skip the most important questions, and unqualified leads ended up in the CRM. Splitting the call into clear stages fixed it.

Rules in a prompt aren't guarantees. Ours says one question per turn, and when we reviewed real calls, the agent still asked two at once. You have to keep listening to calls.

What must the AI never say?

Anything about earnings, income or returns. Under the FTC Franchise Rule, a spoken statement about what someone could earn counts as a financial performance representation. If it isn't in Item 19 of your FDD, it's a violation.

Our agent is told never to discuss prices, returns or financial benefits, and leaves those to a specialist. Test yours by asking it directly.

Is AI calling legal?

It can be, with the right setup. This isn't legal advice, so have an attorney review yours. Our compliance guide goes deeper, but these are the three that matter most.

Open every call by saying it's an AI and that it's recorded, so nobody has to ask.

How long does it take, and what does it cost?

Our first version was live in one week, connected to GoHighLevel, Facebook and the website. We've kept refining it ever since, with no fixed schedule.

Running it costs very little. The voice minutes are the main bill, plus an automation tool. The CRM was already there.

Retell lists $0.07 to $0.31 a minute, depending on the setup. Phone numbers, branded caller ID and automation are extra. Compare that to a single $351 lead.

How should the CRM be set up?

Every lead needs a source, an owner, a consent record and a next step, all in one CRM. Otherwise automation just moves a mess around faster. See one CRM for every lead source.

Write each answer from the call into its own field, then check those fields. In our review, a credit score of about 680 was saved as 700, and "next few months" was saved as 2027.

What about no-shows?

Booked isn't finished. When someone misses a meeting, automated texts and emails try to rebook them, along with useful information about the brand they picked.

Show rate at our firm went from 32% to 41%. Booked appointments went from an average of 89 a month to 163. Those are monthly averages from the CRM, for consultant network leads, and other things changed too. The case study has the detail.

We haven't measured how many no-shows come back because of those messages.

What about old leads?

We haven't run a campaign on old leads yet, so this is guidance only. Start with email, and only call once someone says yes. Older consent often never mentioned AI voice, and phone numbers get reassigned.

Quick answers

Should you call, text or email a new franchise lead first?

We call first, because calls get picked up more and qualify people faster. Many vendors recommend text first. We have not run a controlled test of the two.

How many times should you call a franchise lead?

At least three. In our data, 30% of the people we reached only answered on a later attempt.

What percentage of AI calls reach a live person?

In our 5,379 calls, 37% reached a live person, 40% reached voicemail and 23% did not connect.

How long does it take to build an AI voice agent for franchise leads?

Our first version was live in one week, connected to GoHighLevel, Facebook and the website. We have refined it continuously since.

How much does an AI voice agent cost to run?

Our calls averaged about 177 minutes a month. At list prices of $0.07 to $0.31 a minute, that is about $12 to $55 a month in voice minutes, plus phone numbers and automation tools.
