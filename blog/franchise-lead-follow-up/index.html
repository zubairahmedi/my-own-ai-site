Franchise Lead Follow-Up: A System That Doesn't Depend on Memory

By Zehaan Khan · 2026-11-08 · 3 min read

Franchise leads rarely go cold because a rep doesn't care. They go cold because a rep got busy, a task got buried, and nobody noticed the candidate only ever got one touch.

The fix isn't asking people to try harder. It's a follow-up system that doesn't depend on anyone remembering.

One touch is expensive

The 2026 Annual Franchise Development Report puts the average cost per lead at $351 and leads to signed deals at 2%. That means roughly 50 leads for every deal, and about 24 weeks from lead to agreement.

A long sales cycle means candidates need more than one conversation. Yet FranFunnel's study of 530 brands found 73% never sent a text at all.

Candidates who don't pick up the first call aren't necessarily out. They may just be at work.

What a working follow-up system does

We built this for the firm in our case study. It comes down to five rules.

1. Every lead has a next step. No lead sits in the CRM without a scheduled action or a closed reason. A lead with nothing scheduled is a lead you've quietly dropped.

2. The candidate's words set the next step. If they say "call me Thursday," the system books that callback. In the case study, 1,385 calls ended with a callback request and 826 ended with someone giving an appointment time.

3. Channels work together. A missed call triggers a text. A text with no reply gets an email. Nobody gets three of the same message.

4. Booked isn't finished. Reminders run before every meeting, and a no-show triggers a re-book attempt. Show rate in the case study went from 32% to 41%.

5. It knows when to stop. Opt-outs are honored right away and synced everywhere. Candidates who go quiet move out of the active sequence.

A starting cadence

This is a sensible default rather than a published standard. Test it against your own numbers.

When Touch
Minutes after the inquiry Call if consent allows, otherwise email plus a human call task
Same day Text if there was no answer
Day 1 Second call attempt at a different time of day
Day 3 Email with one useful thing, such as territory availability
Day 5 Call and text
Day 8 Email from the named development rep
Day 14 Last call, then a clear "should I close this out?" message

Two limits apply no matter what cadence you use. We cap call attempts at 3 in any rolling 24 hours, and we keep calls inside a set window in the lead's local time.

Old leads need more care

Going back through your dormant leads is usually where the money is. It's also where the risk is.

Older leads may have consented under language that never mentioned AI voice. Phone numbers get reassigned. In Canada, implied consent from an inquiry only lasts 6 months.

So start reactivation with email, and only move someone to a call once they've said yes.

How to find your gap

Pull a list of every lead from the last 18 months and count three things:

  1. Leads with zero logged calls
  2. Leads with exactly one touch
  3. Leads with no activity in more than 30 days

Those three numbers tell you how much you've already paid for and never worked. We call this a CRM leak audit, and the AI RevOps Engine does the counting automatically.
