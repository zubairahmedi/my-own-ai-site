We've been building AI automation since 2023 and franchise lead systems since 2024. This is how we built the AI voice agent behind our case study, including the parts that went wrong.

Disclosure: we're a certified Retell AI partner, so take that into account. You can see it on Zehaan's LinkedIn.

What the agent's job is

The agent has a narrow job. It reaches a new franchise lead, finds out if they're serious and books the next step. It doesn't sell the franchise.

The stack

How a call flows

If you're building this, trigger step 5 on Retell's call_analyzed event. At call_ended, the transcript and analysis may not be ready yet.

Pull answers into fields

The most useful thing we did was treat every call as data. A transcript sitting in a notes field helps nobody. An "Available Capital" field can be filtered, routed and reported on.

Define your post-call fields before you write the prompt. Then write the prompt to collect those answers.

What the data showed

The 41-second average covers every call, including the ones that didn't connect and the voicemails. Live calls of a minute or more typically ran about 2.4 minutes.

Retell lists its voice agents at $0.07 to $0.31 a minute, depending on the models you choose.

The rules we build around

An AI caller needs consent that covers AI voice, needs to say it's an AI, and can't talk earnings. Our compliance guide covers all of it, and you should have an attorney review your setup.

Is it worth building?

For this firm, booked appointments went from an average of 89 a month to 163, and show rate from 32% to 41%. This was consultant network leads, and other things changed too, so read the full case study before you draw conclusions.
