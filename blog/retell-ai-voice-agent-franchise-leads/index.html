How We Built an AI Voice Agent for Franchise Leads With Retell AI

By Zehaan Khan · 2026-11-08 · 4 min read

We've been building AI automation since 2023 and franchise lead systems since 2024. This is how we built the AI voice agent behind our case study, including the parts that went wrong.

Disclosure: we're a certified Retell AI partner, so take that into account. You can see it on Zehaan's LinkedIn.

What the agent's job is

The agent has a narrow job. It reaches a new franchise lead, finds out if they're serious and books the next step. It doesn't sell the franchise.

On every call, it tries to capture:

  • Investment range, in bands
  • Self-reported credit
  • The franchise they're interested in
  • Veteran status, for discount programs
  • When they want to buy
  • The next step: book, call back or text

The stack

Piece What it does
Facebook ads, website forms, feeds Where leads come from
GoHighLevel The CRM, where every lead lands
Make Connects the CRM to the calling platform
Retell AI Runs the voice agent
GoHighLevel workflows Follow-up after the call

How a call flows

  1. A new lead lands in GoHighLevel from any source.
  2. A workflow checks the lead and triggers the call through Make.
  3. Retell places the call, and the agent has the conversation.
  4. When the call is analyzed, Retell sends a webhook with the result.
  5. Make writes each answer into its own CRM field.
  6. A tag starts the follow-up workflow, based on what the person said.

If you're building this, trigger step 5 on Retell's call_analyzed event. At call_ended, the transcript and analysis may not be ready yet.

Pull answers into fields

The most useful thing we did was treat every call as data. A transcript sitting in a notes field helps nobody. An "Available Capital" field can be filtered, routed and reported on.

Define your post-call fields before you write the prompt. Then write the prompt to collect those answers.

What the data showed

Measure Result
Calls placed by the AI 5,379
Unique leads worked 1,733
Connect rate 77%
Live talk time 61.8 hours
Average call length About 41 seconds
Latency About 1.3 to 1.4 seconds

The average call is short. That number includes quick hang-ups and fast no's. So don't expect every call to be a deep conversation.

Retell lists its voice agents at $0.07 to $0.31 a minute, depending on the models you choose.

What went wrong

We counted calls as people. Our first reports said 1,385 people asked for a callback. Those were calls, and one person can ask twice. We fixed the language everywhere.

We claimed too much coverage. Early copy said the AI called every lead. It worked 1,733 unique leads, while roughly 750 arrived each month. We dropped that claim.

A dated comparison flattered us. Our 2024 lead count included a one-time CRM import, so we stopped comparing lead volume and conversion rate.

The platform changes under you. Retell retired POST /v2/list-calls on June 15, 2026. The replacement, /v3/list-calls, returns results inside an items object, which can break any scenario that expects a plain list. Read deprecation notices, or your reports will quietly stop.

We still haven't measured seconds to first dial. That needs each call matched to the lead's created time, and we haven't finished it. So we don't publish a number.

The rules we build around

An AI caller needs consent that covers AI voice, needs to say it's an AI, and can't talk earnings. Our compliance guide covers all of it, and you should have an attorney review your setup.

Is it worth building?

For this firm, booked appointments went from an average of 89 a month to 163, and show rate from 32% to 41%. This was consultant network leads, and other things changed too, so read the full case study before you draw conclusions.
