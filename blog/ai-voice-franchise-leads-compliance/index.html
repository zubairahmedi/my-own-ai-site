AI Voice Calling for Franchise Leads: The Rules That Matter

By Zehaan Khan · 2026-11-08 · 4 min read

The biggest legal risk of putting an AI on the phone with franchise candidates isn't robocall law. It's the AI saying a number.

This is how we think about it when we build, including for the firm in our case study. It isn't legal advice, and you should have a franchise attorney and a TCPA attorney review your setup.

Rule 1: The AI can't talk earnings

Under the FTC Franchise Rule, any statement of a specific level or range of sales, income or profit counts as a financial performance representation, including a spoken one. If it isn't in Item 19 of your FDD, it's a violation. The rule reaches anyone involved in selling the franchise.

Now imagine an AI making one loose income comment across thousands of calls. Candidates will ask "how much can I make?" So the AI has to be built to refuse, hand off to a person and log the question. Test it regularly, because no AI is perfect.

Rule 2: AI voice needs the right consent

The FCC ruled in February 2024 that AI-generated voices count as artificial voices under the TCPA. A franchise sales call is telemarketing, so you need consent that covers AI or prerecorded calls from your brand, and you need to be able to prove it for each lead.

Courts are still working through parts of this, so we build to the stricter reading.

Portal and broker leads are the trap. Their consent usually names the portal, not your brand. Our approach:

  • Add a consent checkbox to your own form that names your brand and AI voice calls
  • Send portal and broker leads an instant email with a one-tap "call me now" link
  • Have the AI call only after that yes, and otherwise use a human call task

Rule 3: Say it's an AI, in the first sentence

Some states, including Maine, Utah and California, have disclosure rules that reach certain AI interactions. About a dozen states also require everyone on a call to agree to recording.

One opening line covers both: say that it's an AI, and that the call is recorded. If someone asks whether they're talking to a real person, the AI never denies it.

Rule 4: Respect the clock

The calling window we use is Monday to Friday 9am to 8pm and Saturday 10am to 6pm, in the lead's local time, with no more than 3 call attempts in a rolling 24 hours.

A lead that arrives at night gets an instant email, then a call when the window opens. This does break the "we call everyone in 60 seconds" pitch. We'd rather be honest about speed.

Rule 5: Make opting out easy

People can revoke consent by any reasonable means, and the FCC expects it to be honored within 10 business days. We sync it right away across every channel.

Rule 6: Be careful with old leads

Dead-lead reactivation is the riskiest thing on this list. Old consent often never mentioned AI voice, and numbers get reassigned. In Canada, implied consent from an inquiry lasts 6 months.

Start with email. Move to phone only after someone says yes.

Rule 7: Qualify and schedule, never sell

Broker registration laws are expanding, and they can reach anyone paid in connection with selling a franchise. So our AI asks questions and books meetings. It doesn't pitch the investment or negotiate. We also charge flat fees, never a share of a franchise award.

A quick checklist

  • [ ] Does your form's consent name your brand and AI voice?
  • [ ] Do you store the exact consent wording for each lead?
  • [ ] Is there a separate path for portal and broker leads?
  • [ ] Does the AI disclose itself and the recording right away?
  • [ ] Have you tested it against earnings questions?
  • [ ] Are calling windows set by the lead's time zone?
  • [ ] Does an opt-out reach every channel?
  • [ ] Has an attorney reviewed all of this?
