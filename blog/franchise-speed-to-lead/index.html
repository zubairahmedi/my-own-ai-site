Franchise Speed to Lead: What the Data Actually Says

By Zehaan Khan · 2026-11-08 · 4 min read

The short version

  • Most brands are slow. 28% of 530 never replied to a web inquiry at all.
  • Speed wins the conversation, not the deal. The famous stats measure contact and qualifying.
  • Nobody can legally AI-call everyone in 60 seconds. Consent and calling hours come first.

On this page

  • How fast franchise brands really respond
  • The stats people misquote
  • Why "call every lead in 60 seconds" is a bad promise
  • How we built the first touch

Most franchise brands are slow to answer a new inquiry, and a lot of them never answer at all. That part is well documented. What gets exaggerated is how much speed alone will do for you.

Here's what the published numbers say, where people misquote them, and how we built the first touch in our case study.

How fast franchise brands really respond

Two groups test this in franchise development specifically.

FranFunnel sent one real web inquiry to each of 530 brands in spring 2025:

ServiceScore's 2025 mystery shop tested 102 brands and found that 44% called the prospect. Across its yearly shops, only about 4 to 5 in 10 brands ever pick up the phone for a web inquiry.

Now put that next to the cost. The 2026 Annual Franchise Development Report puts the average cost per lead at $351 in 2025, up from $271 a year earlier. Paying that and then not calling is the expensive part.

The stats people misquote

You've probably heard that responding in 5 minutes makes you 21 times more likely to convert. That isn't what the study found.

A lead response vendor was behind both studies, so read them with that in mind.

So the honest version is this: being fast gets you the conversation. It doesn't sign the deal. A franchise sale still takes about 24 weeks on average.

Why "call every lead in 60 seconds" is a bad promise

A lot of tools sell this. It has two problems.

Consent. The FCC treats AI voices as artificial voices under the TCPA, so an AI sales call needs consent that covers it. Portal and broker leads usually consented to the portal, not your brand. We go through this in our AI voice compliance guide.

Hours. A lead who fills in a form at 11pm shouldn't get a robot calling at 11:01pm.

How we built the first touch

For the firm in our case study, a new lead gets called and every answer gets written back to the CRM, so the rep starts with the full picture. On new builds, we put this consent and calling hours check in front of the call.

Across 5,379 calls, the AI reached a live person 37% of the time and voicemail another 40%. It handled 48.5 hours of live conversation that nobody on the team had to dial.

We haven't published an average seconds-to-first-dial number yet, because we haven't finished matching every call to the time each lead was created. When we have it, we'll publish it.
