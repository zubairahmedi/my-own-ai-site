The Franchise Development Dashboard: What to Track and Why

By Zehaan Khan · 2026-11-08 · 3 min read

A good franchise development dashboard answers one question: where are candidates getting stuck, and whose pipeline is it in?

For the firm in our case study, we built one that pulls leads, appointments, referrals and sources by consultant on an automatic schedule. Nobody builds a report by hand anymore. Here's what we'd put on yours.

The numbers worth tracking

Metric How to define it Published benchmark
Cost per lead Spend divided by new leads, by source $351 in 2025
Time to first touch Inquiry to first real human or AI contact 9.1 hours average first email reply
Ever phoned Share of leads with at least one call 44% of brands called a mystery shopper
Lead to appointment Leads who book an intro call Track your own
Show rate Booked meetings that actually happen Track your own
Touches in 14 days Calls, texts and emails per lead Track your own
Stale leads Open leads with no activity in 30 days Aim for zero
Lead to agreement Signed deals over leads 2%, or 1.5% on FranConnect data

The AFDR and FranConnect figures measure different groups in different ways, so compare yourself to each one separately. Don't average them.

Show rate and lead to appointment are the two we weight most early on, because you see them in weeks. A signed deal takes about 24 weeks to show up.

Show every number by consultant

Totals hide the most useful information. In our work, one person's book can look completely different from the rest of the team's once you split it out.

Rep-level views should show leads assigned, first-touch time, appointments, show rate and deals. The point isn't to punish anyone. Usually you'll find something to copy from your best performer.

Four ways dashboards go wrong

Counting calls as people. Our call data showed 1,385 callback requests, and those are calls. One person can ask twice. We say so in the case study, because the difference matters.

Comparing against an import. If you loaded old contacts into your CRM one month, that month's lead count is fake. Our 2024 data had this problem, so we dropped the comparison.

Missing calendars. We found one rep's appointments were being under-counted because of how a personal calendar was set up. If a rep's numbers look oddly low, check the plumbing before you have the conversation.

Empty outcome fields. If nobody records whether a meeting happened, your show rate is a guess. Make the outcome required.

How ours is built

The setup is simple. Scheduled n8n workflows pull from the CRM and calendars, calculate the numbers per consultant and store the results. The dashboard reads from that store.

Doing the math in code means the same question always gets the same answer. If two people pull a number and get two results, the team stops trusting the dashboard.

None of this works without every source in one CRM.

Where a dashboard stops

A dashboard only answers the questions you thought to build a chart for. The biggest leaks tend to be in questions nobody asked, such as which qualified candidates never got a meeting.

That's the job of the AI RevOps Engine, which reads the same data and lets you ask anything.
